This week was about mapping. I don't think I realized how complex map-making was before this week. The GIS Lab was very challenging, but my classmates and I managed to make a map showing historical sites in Ontario that were close to rivers. I think I might want to return to the GIS lab for my PDAP. Since it was such a complicated process, unfortunately I do not think I would be able to do that on my own. But I hope I would be able to speak to one of the professionals that were helping us today to make my map, or talk through what making one would look like.
In the Skill Building Workshop, I learned about the many variations of maps that can be created using one set of data. You can focus on information by sex, by date of death, by proximity to youth burials, etc. 
I learned a similar idea in the reading: "Archaeology, Crafting Maps and Political Change." The example of Helen Wickstead and Janet Hodgson's 2009 Feminist map stood out to me. It was almost like a performance, kind of like how the Piraye Hacıgüzeller article suggested. They told a very intricate story through mapping. They were able to remain creative in their process still which was also quite inspiring to me as I find that exercises like these can often be very linear and hard to make meaningful.
